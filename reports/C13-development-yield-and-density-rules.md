# C13: Development yield and density rules

Researched 2026-10-30. Brief file unavailable; scope taken from the task line. The shared web-search budget ran out part-way through, so Saudi, Abu Dhabi community-facility and yield-tool sources were reached only through direct links on publisher sites; see Risks.

## Answer

Studio should compute yield through jurisdiction rule profiles, not one GFA (gross floor area) formula. Tag every space in the massing with a category (parking, plant, shaft, balcony, void, and so on) and its level. Then let a profile decide five things: what is included, which wall line is used (exterior face, middle of wall or internal face), whether shafts count once or on every floor, what area the parking rates use (net area, GFA, GIA or units) and whether the rates are minimums, maximums or a range. The differences are large. I measured one 5,000 m2 mixed-use tower and podium in Python. The FAR (floor area ratio) is 8.00 under the Dubai Building Code. Under Abu Dhabi's Development Code it is 8.53 or 10.53, because two clauses of that code disagree about podium parking. It is 9.29 under Singapore's URA rules, or 10.50 once the extra parking counts as GFA. Saudi rules control site coverage, not FAR, and the Saudi "total building area" gives an equivalent of 11.69. London GEA (gross external area) gives 12.79. Efficiency (32,840 m2 of sellable and lettable area divided by GFA) runs from 82% to 51%. Required parking is at least 494 bays in Dubai and 655 in Saudi Arabia, 581 under Abu Dhabi's CBD rates, at most 0 or 368 in London, and 274 to 352 in Singapore. Population is 760 people at the Dubai lift-design rates, or 928 bedspaces under England's national space standard. The main caveat: in Dubai the plot's affection plan and the master developer's DCR (development control regulations) override the code. Several sources are also old (Abu Dhabi parking rates, 2009) or could not be reached: Abu Dhabi's Community Facility Planning Standards, the Saudi Building Code text, DDA DCRs and RERA's measurement rules. Every rule in a profile therefore needs a source, an edition date and a way for the user to override it.

## Detail

### GFA definitions: what is included and excluded

**Dubai.** The Dubai Building Code (DBC) 2021 is the city-wide reference. Decree 45 of 2021 applies it "throughout the Emirate, including in Special Development Zones and free zones" [[3]](https://dlp.dubai.gov.ae/Legislation%20Reference/2021/Decree%20No.%20%2845%29%20of%202021%20Concerning%20the%20Dubai%20Building%20Code.html), and Dubai Municipality still lists the 2021 edition [[2]](https://www.dm.gov.ae/municipality-business/planning-and-construction/dubai-building-code-2/). Table A.3 includes habitable space, mezzanines, lobbies, corridors, stairs, storage above ground and the base of atria. It excludes parking, parking lobbies, internal roads, loading, mechanical and service rooms, ducts, shafts, refuse chutes, waste rooms, storage below ground, prayer rooms, pools and their services, open arcades, voids, terraces and balconies. A rooftop gym is excluded up to 50% of the roof area, and the lift shaft counts only at the recall floor [[1]](https://dmpmedia.dm.gov.ae/uploads/2021/12/Dubai%20Building%20Code_English_2021%20Edition_compressed.pdf#page=13). The code also says GFA "might be in development control regulations (DCR)" and that consultants use the DBC "unless GFA is stated in the DCR" [[1]](https://dmpmedia.dm.gov.ae/uploads/2021/12/Dubai%20Building%20Code_English_2021%20Edition_compressed.pdf#page=12). That sentence points to "Table A.1", but the GFA table is Table A.3 [[1]](https://dmpmedia.dm.gov.ae/uploads/2021/12/Dubai%20Building%20Code_English_2021%20Edition_compressed.pdf#page=12)[[1]](https://dmpmedia.dm.gov.ae/uploads/2021/12/Dubai%20Building%20Code_English_2021%20Edition_compressed.pdf#page=13). The same code defines three more areas: built-up area, which adds balconies, covered parking, basements and service floors [[1]](https://dmpmedia.dm.gov.ae/uploads/2021/12/Dubai%20Building%20Code_English_2021%20Edition_compressed.pdf#page=5); gross area (GA), inside the exterior walls; and net area (NA), which leaves out corridors, stairs, toilets and mechanical rooms [[1]](https://dmpmedia.dm.gov.ae/uploads/2021/12/Dubai%20Building%20Code_English_2021%20Edition_compressed.pdf#page=5)[[1]](https://dmpmedia.dm.gov.ae/uploads/2021/12/Dubai%20Building%20Code_English_2021%20Edition_compressed.pdf#page=6).

**Abu Dhabi.** The Abu Dhabi Capital Development Code (DMT, edition dated September 2022) gives explicit include and exclude lists in section 603.02 [[8]](https://www.dmt.gov.ae/-/media/Project/DMT/DMT/E-Library/Abu-Dhabi-Capital-Development-Code.pdf#page=176). Included: atriums, shafts and lift shafts counted once; stairwells on every floor; "storage and equipment spaces"; any covered space with headroom of 2.1 m or more; covered balconies enclosed on three sides; enclosed rooftop habitable space; habitable space at and below grade; "serviced" parking structures at and above grade; and rooftop enclosures over 25% of the roof. Excluded: space open to the sky; projecting balconies; below-grade parking, loading and access; public sky-bridges; rooftop enclosures up to 25% of the roof; transformer rooms; mechanical floors; "un-serviced" parking structures; and colonnades open on three sides [[8]](https://www.dmt.gov.ae/-/media/Project/DMT/DMT/E-Library/Abu-Dhabi-Capital-Development-Code.pdf#page=176). The code does not define "serviced", and every residential district I sampled adds note N-25, "The Podium Parking floor does not count towards the building GFA" [[8]](https://www.dmt.gov.ae/-/media/Project/DMT/DMT/E-Library/Abu-Dhabi-Capital-Development-Code.pdf#page=35).

**Saudi Arabia.** The Ministry's 2024 residential building requirements (اشتراطات إنشاء المباني السكنية) define total building area as the sum of the floor areas of all roofed buildings. They exclude open light wells and internal courts, open canopies, mezzanine floors and underground car-park floors [[12]](https://momah.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2024-07/ashtratat%20a%27nsha%20almbany%20alsknyt%20m%60%20alqrar.pdf#page=11). A basement is a floor whose ceiling top is at most 1.20 m above the pavement in front of the main entrance [[12]](https://momah.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2024-07/ashtratat%20a%27nsha%20almbany%20alsknyt%20m%60%20alqrar.pdf#page=10). The document does not say where to measure walls or how to treat balconies, shafts or plant. I could not read the Saudi Building Code (SBC 201) definitions. The national code site lists SBC 201 as free, but its viewer did not load here [[16]](https://sbc.gov.sa/Ar/BC/Pages/buildingcode/BC.aspx?year=2024).

**London.** The London Plan uses RICS measures rather than its own GFA. GEA includes perimeter walls, internal walls, stairwells, lift-wells, plant rooms (even above the roof), loading bays and garages. It excludes open-sided balconies, canopies, open parking and roof terraces [[19]](https://www.rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/standards/May_2015_Code_Of_Measuring_Practice_6th_Edition.pdf#page=11). GIA is the same but measured to the internal face of the perimeter walls [[19]](https://www.rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/standards/May_2015_Code_Of_Measuring_Practice_6th_Edition.pdf#page=15). RICS GIA counts areas with headroom under 1.5 m (item 2.14) [[19]](https://www.rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/standards/May_2015_Code_Of_Measuring_Practice_6th_Edition.pdf#page=15). For dwellings, the London Plan and the NDSS (Nationally Described Space Standard) exclude them from GIA "unless used solely for storage" [[17]](https://www.london.gov.uk/sites/default/files/the_london_plan_2021.pdf#page=141)[[18]](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6123c60e8fa8f53dd1f9b04d/160519_Nationally_Described_Space_Standard.pdf).

**Singapore.** URA counts "all (a) covered floor area ... (b) uncovered area used for commercial purposes, and (c) strata area" [[24]](https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/gross-floor-area/), each with its own topic page. Parking lots, driveways, refuse chutes and loading bays beside a driveway are excluded [[26]](https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/gross-floor-area/car-parks-motorcycle-parks-and-related-facilities/)[[32]](https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/gross-floor-area/refuse-chambers-and-refuse-chutes/)[[33]](https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/gross-floor-area/loading-and-unloading-bays/). Balconies, walls, columns and void decks are included [[28]](https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/gross-floor-area/balconies/)[[35]](https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/gross-floor-area/walls-and-columns/)[[36]](https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/gross-floor-area/void-decks/). Lift shafts count once at the lowest floor [[29]](https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/gross-floor-area/lift-shaft/). The 2023 harmonisation moved every agency to the middle of the wall and took voids out of strata area [[25]](https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/circulars/dc22-09/).

### FAR and plot ratio

All three ratio-based systems divide GFA by plot area, but the numerator differs (D1 to D8), and so does the denominator. Abu Dhabi's definition uses "net plot area", while 603.03 says "total plot area" and then divides by net plot area [[8]](https://www.dmt.gov.ae/-/media/Project/DMT/DMT/E-Library/Abu-Dhabi-Capital-Development-Code.pdf#page=171)[[8]](https://www.dmt.gov.ae/-/media/Project/DMT/DMT/E-Library/Abu-Dhabi-Capital-Development-Code.pdf#page=176). Singapore uses the area "measured between the survey boundary lines" [[24]](https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/gross-floor-area/). In Dubai, the control usually arrives as an absolute GFA on the plot's site plan. A Trakhees-issued site plan for a Nakheel plot reads "GFA=4804.84 Sq. m, FAR=3.5" on 1,372.81 m2, with coverage "Podium: 100%, Tower: 60%". It refers parking to the Trakhees regulations and the master developer's DCR [[6]](https://www.cgidubai.gov.in/content/Affection-plan-Updated%20-IC1-CTYL-16_013_12.05.2026.pdf). A DHCC plot sheet gives "MAXIMUM GFA 23,851.72 m2" on 7,907.01 m2 and refers parking to the DBC [[7]](https://www.dhcc.ae/gallery/dhcc-development-guidelines-phase-2.pdf). Saudi residential requirements control coverage per floor and the number of floors, not FAR [[12]](https://momah.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2024-07/ashtratat%20a%27nsha%20almbany%20alsknyt%20m%60%20alqrar.pdf#page=17)[[12]](https://momah.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2024-07/ashtratat%20a%27nsha%20almbany%20alsknyt%20m%60%20alqrar.pdf#page=22). The ministry's own English translation of its Office Requirements defines "Floor Area Ratio" as the ground-floor built-up area divided by site area, which is a coverage, and says the Arabic prevails [[13]](https://momah.gov.sa/sites/default/files/2026-10/%D8%A7%D8%B4%D8%AA%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%B7%D8%A7%D8%AA%20%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%83%D8%A7%D8%AA%D8%A8%20%28English%20Version%29_0.pdf). London sets no FAR limit in the policies I read. It asks major applications to report FAR (GEA / site area) and Site Coverage Ratio, plus units, habitable rooms, bedrooms and bedspaces per hectare [[17]](https://www.london.gov.uk/sites/default/files/the_london_plan_2021.pdf#page=131). Singapore adds bonus GFA on top of the Master Plan GPR, capped at 10% [[24]](https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/gross-floor-area/).

### Efficiency ratios (net saleable or leasable to GFA)

No source I opened sets a target efficiency for apartments or offices. The only quoted norms are Abu Dhabi's mall rule ("The GLA can be a minimum of 80% of the GFA") [[9]](https://admobility.gov.ae/-/media/files/the-approved-technical-requirements-table.pdf) and Singapore's cap of 15% of a unit's internal nett area for balconies [[37]](https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/residential/flats-condominiums/balconies-pes-prt/). Efficiency is therefore a result, and it moves with the denominator. The worked example below measures it: 82.1% on Dubai GFA against 51.4% on London GEA for the same building and the same 32,840 m2 numerator. On a typical tower floor (860 m2 of apartments) it ranges from 70.5% (Saudi, which counts shafts and balconies) to 82.9% (Dubai). The numerator is not standard either. The DBC net area excludes toilets and mechanical rooms [[1]](https://dmpmedia.dm.gov.ae/uploads/2021/12/Dubai%20Building%20Code_English_2021%20Edition_compressed.pdf#page=6), RICS NIA excludes common areas and plant [[19]](https://www.rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/standards/May_2015_Code_Of_Measuring_Practice_6th_Edition.pdf#page=19), and the Dubai sales measure (RERA) was not found.

### Occupancy per unit type

Only two sources give per-unit rates, and they measure different things. The DBC sizes lifts on 1.5 persons for a studio, 1.8 for a one-bed, 3 for a two-bed, 4 for a three-bed and 5 for a four-bed, plus 1 per extra bedroom or live-in housekeeper room (Table D.5) [[1]](https://dmpmedia.dm.gov.ae/uploads/2021/12/Dubai%20Building%20Code_English_2021%20Edition_compressed.pdf#page=272). Staff and student housing use 2, 2, 4 and 6 [[1]](https://dmpmedia.dm.gov.ae/uploads/2021/12/Dubai%20Building%20Code_English_2021%20Edition_compressed.pdf#page=274)[[1]](https://dmpmedia.dm.gov.ae/uploads/2021/12/Dubai%20Building%20Code_English_2021%20Edition_compressed.pdf#page=275). For exit design the DBC uses floor area per person instead, for example 5.0 m2 net per person in shared sleeping space. It notes that Table B.2 departs from the UAE fire code only for residential [[1]](https://dmpmedia.dm.gov.ae/uploads/2021/12/Dubai%20Building%20Code_English_2021%20Edition_compressed.pdf#page=56). England's NDSS classifies homes by bedspaces, for example 1b2p at 50 m2 and 3b6p at 95 m2 for single-storey dwellings [[18]](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6123c60e8fa8f53dd1f9b04d/160519_Nationally_Described_Space_Standard.pdf). It says this "does not imply actual occupancy" [[18]](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6123c60e8fa8f53dd1f9b04d/160519_Nationally_Described_Space_Standard.pdf). Abu Dhabi's mosque rules estimate worshippers as 35% of the population [[10]](https://www.dmt.gov.ae/-/media/Project/DMT/DMT/E-Library/10-24/02/Abu-Dhabi-Mosque-Development-Regulations---Volume-1--Planning-En.pdf#page=27) but give no persons per unit. I found no Abu Dhabi, Saudi or Singapore rate. Singapore instead caps the number of units: the divisor is 85 m2 outside the Central Area and 100 m2 in its Maps 2 to 10, with no cap in Map 1. The formula is published only as an image, which I could not read [[38]](https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/development-control/development-control-handbooks/residential/flats-condominiums/maximum-number-du/).

### Worked example (measured)

**Method.** [yield_compare.py](assets/C13/yield_compare.py) defines one 5,000 m2 plot (100 m by 50 m) and one building. Each floor lists internal component areas plus an external wall band (perimeter × 0.30 m, less the corners). Each rule set returns, per component, whether it is counted. The wall band follows the rule's measuring line, in proportion to the counted share of the floor. Run it with `python3 yield_compare.py --floors`; the full output is in [run_output.md](assets/C13/run_output.md) and the numbers in [results.json](assets/C13/results.json).

**What the numbers show.** One building gives FAR from 8.00 to 12.79, a 60% spread. The single largest within-code swing is Abu Dhabi's podium parking: about 10,000 m2, or 2.0 FAR, depending on which clause applies. The Dubai built-up area is 1.63 times the Dubai GFA. Over-providing parking, which is safe in Dubai, is a GFA penalty in Singapore, a breach in London and still a shortfall in Saudi Arabia and Abu Dhabi.

### Measurement standards relevant to the region

- **RICS Code of Measuring Practice, 6th edition** (effective May 2015) defines GEA, GIA and NIA [[19]](https://www.rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/standards/May_2015_Code_Of_Measuring_Practice_6th_Edition.pdf#page=11)[[19]](https://www.rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/standards/May_2015_Code_Of_Measuring_Practice_6th_Edition.pdf#page=15)[[19]](https://www.rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/standards/May_2015_Code_Of_Measuring_Practice_6th_Edition.pdf#page=19). RICS says members should keep using the 6th edition "and/or IPMS: All Buildings" until the 7th is final. The page gives 13 October 2026 as the closing date of the consultation on the 7th edition draft, and RICS Property Measurement 2nd edition is archived [[20]](https://www.rics.org/profession-standards/rics-standards-and-guidance/sector-standards/real-estate-standards/code-of-measuring-practice)[[21]](https://www.rics.org/profession-standards/rics-standards-and-guidance/sector-standards/real-estate-standards/rics-property-measurement-2nd-edition). The measurement basis used must be recorded [[20]](https://www.rics.org/profession-standards/rics-standards-and-guidance/sector-standards/real-estate-standards/code-of-measuring-practice).
- **IPMS: All Buildings** (IPMS Coalition, January 2023) supersedes the earlier asset-class IPMS standards [[23]](https://ipmsc.org/standards/). It defines IPMS 1 (external), IPMS 2 (internal), IPMS 3.1/3.2 (exclusive use) and IPMS 4.1/4.2 (selected areas). It also defines component areas A to H, for example B1 vertical circulation, C technical areas and G2 car parking [[22]](https://ipmsc.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/ipms-all-buildings-.pdf#page=10). IPMS 1 includes balconies (External Floor Areas) and rooftop plant but must list them separately. It excludes voids except at the lowest level [[22]](https://ipmsc.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/ipms-all-buildings-.pdf#page=19)[[22]](https://ipmsc.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/ipms-all-buildings-.pdf#page=20).
- **Dubai (DLD, RERA).** In April 2014 the Dubai Land Department said "there are no specific standards to measure area" and that it would adopt IPMS [[41]](https://meconstructionnews.com/7178/dubai-property-market-to-get-new-area-measurement-standards) (old, third party). In 2016 RICS's isurv reported IPMS use on Burj 2020 and in DIFC tenancy agreements, and Saudi government engagement [[42]](https://www.isurv.com/info/390/features/10116/ipms_adoption_in_the_middle_east) (old, secondary). In 2018 Gulf News reported that units of entitlement for buildings not yet handed over would follow "upcoming Surveying Regulations" on common and unit area, with a RERA-approved methodology statement [[43]](https://gulfnews.com/business/property/units-of-entitlement-key-1.638397) (third party). I found no current RERA or DLD unit-area rule.
- **Singapore.** Strata area (SLA) and GFA (URA) have been aligned since 1 June 2023: all strata area is GFA, and voids are out of strata area [[25]](https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/circulars/dc22-09/).

### How yield tools let users define GFA and efficiency

- **TestFit.** Public pages say users set "unit mix and unit sizes", build zoning profiles with FAR, dwelling units per acre and a site parking ratio, and compare schemes on "NRSF, efficiency ratio, yield on cost" [[45]](https://www.testfit.io/product/site-solver). TestFit's FAR explainer defines GFA as "the total square footage of all occupiable, enclosed floor space". It says balconies, roof decks, unoccupied basements and mechanical rooms are "usually" excluded and parking "depends on local code" [[44]](https://www.testfit.io/feasibility-tools/floor-area-ratio-calculator). Detailed help sits in a sign-in "Accelerator" [[46]](https://www.testfit.io/customer-success), and help.testfit.io did not resolve from this machine.
- **Giraffe.** Public pages say users "model GFA, NSA" and export area schedules to Excel and Argus [[47]](https://giraffe.build/use-cases/au-residential). The knowledge base's Calculations collection (97 articles) returned HTTP 403, so I could not verify it [[48]](https://help.giraffe.build/en/collections/19736151-calculations).
- **Autodesk Forma.** The public SDK (forma-embedded-view-sdk 0.96.0, `areaMetrics.d.ts`) exposes `builtInMetrics.grossFloorArea` as "Total area of all building model polygons (on all floors)". It also exposes `buildingCoverage`, `siteArea`, user-created `customMetrics` with a per-function breakdown, `unitStatistics.count` and `parkingStatistics` (spots and area) [[49]](https://registry.npmjs.org/forma-embedded-view-sdk/-/forma-embedded-view-sdk-0.96.0.tgz)[[50]](https://app.autodeskforma.com/forma-embedded-view-sdk/docs/index.html). Built-in GFA therefore has no exclusions; exclusions and efficiency have to be custom metrics. The help article it links to needs a Forma login [[51]](https://help.autodeskforma.com/en/articles/6995661-introduction-to-area-metrics).
- **Archistar.** No public documentation on GFA or efficiency was found; its training pages list only general software courses [[52]](https://www.archistar.ai/training)[[53]](https://academy.archistar.ai).

In what I could read, none of the four encodes a Gulf GFA rule. Each treats GFA as gross modelled area plus user-set factors or custom metrics.

### Sketch: a jurisdiction rule profile in C#

A general example, not Studio code. It shows the shape the worked example needs: a category per space, a per-jurisdiction rule, a wall factor and a "count once" switch.

```csharp
using System;
using System.Collections.Generic;
using System.Linq;

public enum SpaceCat { Apartment, Office, Retail, Lobby, Corridor, Stairs, LiftShaft, ServiceShaft,
    RefuseChute, Plant, MepFloor, RoofPlant, Parking, ParkingLobby, Loading, Storage, Void,
    BalconyProjecting, BalconyRecessed }
public enum LevelKind { Basement, Ground, PodiumParking, Podium, Amenity, Tower, Mep, Roof }

public sealed record Space(SpaceCat Cat, double Area, string Use);
public sealed record Floor(string Name, LevelKind Level, double Perimeter, IReadOnlyList<Space> Spaces);

public sealed record GfaProfile(
    string Name, string SourceUrl, DateOnly Edition,
    double WallFactor,                 // 1.0 exterior face, 0.5 wall middle, 0.0 internal face
    bool ShaftsOnce,
    Func<Space, Floor, double> Include);  // 0..1

public static class Gfa
{
    static readonly HashSet<SpaceCat> Once = new() { SpaceCat.LiftShaft, SpaceCat.ServiceShaft, SpaceCat.RefuseChute };

    public static double Compute(IEnumerable<Floor> floors, GfaProfile p, double wallT = 0.30)
    {
        var seen = new HashSet<SpaceCat>();
        double total = 0;
        foreach (var f in floors)
        {
            double inc = 0, interior = f.Spaces.Where(s => s.Cat != SpaceCat.Void).Sum(s => s.Area);
            foreach (var s in f.Spaces)
            {
                double k = p.Include(s, f);
                if (p.ShaftsOnce && Once.Contains(s.Cat) && k > 0 && !seen.Add(s.Cat)) k = 0;
                inc += s.Area * k;
            }
            double band = f.Perimeter * wallT - 4 * wallT * wallT;
            total += inc + (interior > 0 ? band * p.WallFactor * inc / interior : 0);
        }
        return total;
    }
}
```

Measured: I compiled this sketch with the .NET 8 SDK on Linux (not Windows) and fed it the worked-example floors with an "everything but voids" profile. It returns GEA 63,934.6 m2, the same as the Python script.

## Choices made

- **Inferences about Studio** (not verified): Studio is a Windows C# (.NET) urban-design and masterplanning app for the GCC (UAE first, then Saudi Arabia and Qatar). It has 3D massing (from C2), a live Excel link (C7) and parking layout (C12). I describe no internals.
- **Worked example design.** I invented the building so that every rule is exercised: two basements, a parking podium, offices, a podium-roof gym, a 30-storey tower with one plant floor, projecting and recessed balconies and roof plant. Component areas are internal. Apartment areas are measured to the centre of the walls between units.
- **Assumptions:** a 0.30 m external wall everywhere; 30 m2 gross per parking bay; DBC lift recall floor = G; London studios treated as "1-2 bed"; Abu Dhabi CBD and non-CBD both shown; Singapore Zones 2 and 3; London CAZ and outer PTAL 2-3.
- **Rounding:** up per use for minimums (Dubai, Abu Dhabi; Saudi states it); nearest integer per use for Singapore (stated); down for London maximums.
- **Dubai interpretations:** basement stairs and lobbies excluded (only habitable basement space counts); the whole MEP floor excluded, including its stair.
- **Abu Dhabi interpretations:** both readings of podium parking shown; below-grade storage and plant excluded (figure note over A.3); shafts counted once at G; the whole mechanical floor excluded.
- **Saudi interpretations:** whole basement levels excluded as "underground parking floors"; balconies counted as roofed; shafts and plant counted; exterior wall line assumed; the "total building area / plot" ratio shown only for comparison.
- **Singapore interpretations:** roof plant and the LV room counted (no exemption found); enclosed plant on parking floors counted; open service shafts assumed; post-June-2023 definitions used.
- **Dubai population as proxy:** I used it for the Abu Dhabi mosque check because no Abu Dhabi occupancy rate was found, and labelled it.
- **Dubai facility band:** chosen from plot-level density, although the table is meant for districts.
- **Report order:** Choices made placed before What Studio should do, per the coordinator's brief.
